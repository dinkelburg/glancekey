What it does

Your computer asks, your iPhone answers.

Unlock

Unlock your Mac or Linux machine over Wi‑Fi or 5G, at home or away; no Bluetooth or shared Wi‑Fi needed. Pair as many computers as you like, and unlock from the app, Siri, a shortcut or the Action button.

Approve sudo

sudo waits for your iPhone, which shows the command. Tap Approve and look at your phone, or deny it and type your password as usual. Handy when AI agents run on several machines. On Linux, admin dialogs too.

Fill in Mac password prompts

When 1Password, Safari passwords, System Settings or an installer asks for your Mac password, approve it on your iPhone and GlanceKey fills it in.

Set up in a minute, no account

Your iPhone pairs directly with your computer. There’s nothing to sign up for.

  1. Install it on your computer

    The menu bar app on your Mac, or a single program on Linux: use a command below or the download page. Save your login password once; it stays on that computer.

  2. Scan the QR code

    Point your iPhone’s camera at it. Your iPhone creates a key for that computer that only your face can use.

  3. Look at your phone

    From then on, unlocking and approving takes a glance.

brew install --cask dinkelburg/glancekey/glancekey

How it keeps you safe

Your iPhone signs each action and your computer checks it. The relay in between only carries messages.

Your iPhoneAsks, and signs with your face
→↓
RelayPasses messages along, can’t make its own
←↑
Your computerChecks, then unlocks or approves

  • A key only your face can use

    Pairing creates a key inside your iPhone that only works after Face ID recognizes you. Your passcode can’t use it, and adding a new face disables it.

  • Each request works once

    Your computer makes a fresh request for each action, and it expires after a minute. Your iPhone’s answer only fits that one request, so it can’t be copied or used again.

  • Your password stays on your computer

    It’s kept in your Mac’s keychain, or on Linux encrypted to your iPhone, and checked before it’s typed. Typing stops if the screen unlocks halfway through.

  • sudo checks your iPhone itself

    A part of GlanceKey that only the system can change checks your iPhone’s answer, so no other program running as you can approve in your place. Your iPhone signs the same command it showed you.

The apps, the Linux program and the relay are open source. You can run your own relay, and GlanceKey collects no data about you: privacy policy.

Questions

What if my iPhone is dead or stolen?

Type your password as usual. Someone holding your iPhone can’t use GlanceKey either: its key only works after Face ID recognizes your face.

What can the relay see?

Which computer a phone talks to, and the messages in between, such as which sudo command is waiting for approval. It doesn’t store them, apart from what your Lock Screen card shows while it’s on, and it can’t fake an unlock or an approval: those carry your iPhone’s signature, and your computer checks it. Your password and the pairing code never pass through it. Run your own relay if you’d rather not rely on ours.

Does my computer have to be on?

It has to be awake and logged in. After a restart, you type your password once yourself. On power, the Mac app can keep your Mac awake so it stays reachable.

Do I get notifications?

If you want them. When a computer waits for you, a card on your Lock Screen lights up with the request; it also shows your computers and which are locked, with buttons to unlock, lock or approve. Without the card you get a notification that names the computer.

What does it run on?

iPhone with iOS 17 or later, Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later, and Linux with Omarchy, hyprlock, GNOME or KDE.
