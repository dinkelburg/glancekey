Features

Everything that asks for your password

Your computer asks, your iPhone answers. Typing your password becomes the fallback.

Unlock from anywhere

Wi‑Fi, 5G or the other side of the world. Your computer keeps one outgoing connection to a relay, so your iPhone reaches it from any network. No Bluetooth, no shared Wi‑Fi.

Approve sudo with Face ID

sudo waits for your iPhone, which shows the exact command. Tap Approve and look at your phone. Deny it or ignore it and you type your password as usual. On Linux, polkit dialogs too.

Mac password prompts

When 1Password, Safari passwords, System Settings or an installer asks for your Mac password, approve it on your iPhone and GlanceKey fills it in.

All your computers

Pair as many Macs and Linux machines as you like. Opening the app unlocks the one that’s locked. If several are, Unlock all takes a single Face ID.

Siri, Shortcuts, Action button

Unlock without opening the app: ask Siri, run a shortcut or press the Action button.

Made for Linux too

Omarchy, hyprlock, GNOME and KDE. The agent is one static binary that runs as a systemd user service.

How it works

Set up in a minute. No account.

Your iPhone pairs straight with your computer. There’s nothing to sign up for.

  1. Install it on your computer

    The menu bar app on your Mac, or a single binary on Linux. Save your login password once; it stays on that computer.

  2. Scan the QR code

    Point your iPhone’s camera at it. Your iPhone creates a key for that computer, locked behind Face ID.

  3. Unlock with a glance

    Open GlanceKey or press the Action button, look at your phone, and you’re in.

No sign-up, no email address, no password for GlanceKey itself.

Built to be trusted

You shouldn’t have to take our word for it

GlanceKey handles your login password and decides about sudo. So you can read every line, run every part yourself, and we don’t keep anything about you.

Open source

The iPhone app, the Mac app, the Linux agent and the relay are all open source, so you can check exactly what runs on your computer and what runs as root. The code goes public on GitHub at launch.

Self-hostable

The relay is one small Cloudflare Worker. Run it on your own Cloudflare account and point your computers at it; your iPhone follows when you pair. Then nothing of ours is in the loop.

No data collected

No accounts, analytics, ads or tracking. The relay passes messages along without storing them, and your password never leaves your computer. Privacy policy

Security

The relay can’t unlock anything

Every action is signed by a Face ID key on your iPhone and checked by your computer. Whatever sits in between only carries messages.

Your iPhoneSigns with a Face ID key in the Secure Enclave
HTTPS→↓
RelayPasses messages along. Can’t forge or replay them.
Outgoing WebSocket←↑
Your computerChecks every signature. Keeps your password.

  • A key only your face opens

    Pairing creates a key in your iPhone’s Secure Enclave that only signs after Face ID. There’s no passcode fallback, and enrolling a new face invalidates it.

  • Every command is a one-time challenge

    Your computer hands out a single-use code that expires after 60 seconds, and only accepts your iPhone’s signature over that code, the computer, the phone and the action.

  • Your password stays home

    It’s kept in the Mac keychain or the GNOME keyring and checked against your account before it’s typed. Typing stops if the screen unlocks partway through.

  • sudo is checked as root

    A root-owned checker verifies your iPhone’s signature itself, so nothing running as you can approve. Your iPhone signs exactly the command it showed you.

  • Nothing listens on your computer

    Your computers only connect out to the relay. No ports are opened, and the pairing code never passes through the relay.

  • Your password still works

    GlanceKey adds a way in; it never replaces your password. If your phone is out of reach, you type it as always.

FAQ

Questions

Does it need Bluetooth or the same Wi‑Fi?

No. Both devices only need internet. Your computer connects out to the relay, and your iPhone reaches it over HTTPS from any network.

What if my iPhone is dead or stolen?

Type your password, as always. Someone holding your iPhone can’t use GlanceKey either: its key only signs after Face ID recognizes your face.

What can the relay see?

Which computer a phone talks to, and the messages in between, such as which sudo command is waiting for approval. It doesn’t store them and can’t change them: every action carries your iPhone’s signature, and your computer checks it. Your password and the pairing code never pass through it. Run your own relay if you’d rather not rely on ours.

Does my computer have to be on?

It has to be awake and logged in. After a restart, you type your password once yourself. On power, the Mac app can keep your Mac awake so it stays reachable.

Do I get notifications?

If you want them: through the free ntfy app. They only name the computer that’s waiting; the details travel over the relay.

What does it run on?

iPhone with iOS 17 or later, Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later, and Linux with Omarchy, hyprlock, GNOME or KDE.

When can I get it?

GlanceKey is in testing now. The iPhone app is on its way to the App Store, with the Mac app and the Linux agent alongside it.
