Set up GlanceKey

GlanceKey has two parts: the iPhone app, and GlanceKey on each computer. They reach each other through a relay on the internet, so they don't need to be on the same network.

What you need

  • An iPhone with Face ID or Touch ID and iOS 17 or later.
  • A Mac with macOS 14 Sonoma or later, a Linux computer with Omarchy, hyprlock, GNOME or KDE (x86-64 or ARM64), or both.
  • Internet on each of them. No Bluetooth, no shared Wi‑Fi.

iPhone

GlanceKey for iPhone is coming to the App Store; it's in testing now. It learns what it needs to know about a computer when you pair with it.

The app is free for two computers and 15 unlocks and approvals a day, counted on your iPhone. For more, get GlanceKey Unlimited in the app's Settings: €2.99 a month or €24.99 a year. If you reach today's limit, your computers ask for the password as usual, and the count starts again at midnight.

Mac

Install

Download GlanceKey for Mac, open it and drag GlanceKey to your Applications folder. Or use Homebrew:

brew install --cask dinkelburg/glancekey/glancekey

Open it, and GlanceKey appears in the menu bar. Click it to set it up:

  1. Accessibility access: click Grant… and switch GlanceKey on in System Settings. It needs this to type your password on the lock screen and into password dialogs.
  2. Mac password: enter your login password and click Save. GlanceKey checks it, then keeps it in your keychain. It never leaves your Mac. After you change your Mac password, save the new one, and click Trust next to your iPhone once its key matches the one GlanceKey on your iPhone shows on the computer's page.
  3. Pair iPhone… shows a QR code; see Pairing.

The menu also has Keep Mac awake on power adapter (a sleeping Mac can't be reached), Open at login, and Fill in password dialogs from iPhone.

Linux

Install

This installs glancekey-agent in ~/.local/bin and starts it with your session as a systemd user service (without one, it tells you how to start it). It doesn't need root, and running it again updates it.

curl -fsSL https://www.glancekey.com/install.sh | sh

Prefer to do it yourself? Download the program for x86-64 or ARM64, compare it with the checksums, put it on your PATH as glancekey-agent and run glancekey-agent install-service. Homebrew on Linux works too:

brew install dinkelburg/glancekey/glancekey-agent
glancekey-agent install-service

Set up

glancekey-agent set-password   # for Omarchy's lock screen; checked, then encrypted to your iPhone
glancekey-agent pair           # shows a QR code to scan with your iPhone
glancekey-agent status         # the connection, the lock screen and your paired iPhones

How it unlocks depends on your lock screen: on Omarchy it types your saved password (it needs wtype, secret-tool and an unlocked GNOME keyring, which Omarchy has), hyprlock gets a signal to unlock, and GNOME and KDE unlock through logind. Only Omarchy needs the saved password.

Logs: journalctl --user -u glancekey-agent -f

Pairing

On the Mac click Pair iPhone…; on Linux run glancekey-agent pair. Scan the QR code with your iPhone's Camera and GlanceKey opens and pairs. Pairing creates a key on your iPhone for that computer, which only works after Face ID recognizes you. There's no account to make.

Pair each computer once. To add another computer, tap Add another computer in the app. To remove one, use its … menu in the app, and remove the iPhone on the computer too (the Mac menu, or glancekey-agent unpair <name>).

Unlocking

Tap Unlock next to the computer, on the Lock Screen card or in GlanceKey, and look at your iPhone. With several locked, Unlock all in GlanceKey unlocks them with one Face ID. Tap a computer in GlanceKey to lock it or remove it.

Or ask Siri, or use the Unlock Computer shortcut on the Action button, in Control Center or on your Home Screen. It opens GlanceKey and unlocks the locked computer.

The computer has to be awake and logged in. After a restart, type your password once yourself.

Approving sudo

Optional. sudo, and on Linux also polkit's admin dialogs, first waits for your iPhone:

$ sudo pacman -Syu
GlanceKey: approve on your iPhone, or press Enter to type your password (it waits 30 seconds).
GlanceKey: approved on iPhone.

Your iPhone shows the exact command. Tap Approve and look at your phone. If you tap Deny, press Enter in the terminal, don't answer in time, or the iPhone can't be reached, sudo asks for your password as usual. Over SSH it asks for your password straight away.

On the Lock Screen card, Approve approves straight away when the card shows the whole command. A longer command opens in GlanceKey, where you tap Approve after reading it. polkit doesn't say what its admin dialogs are for, so for those your iPhone only shows that a password dialog is waiting; check the dialog on your screen.

It waits 30 seconds unless you choose otherwise: 1, 5 or 15 minutes, or until you answer. Change it on the computer's page in the iPhone app, in the Mac menu (Wait for iPhone), or on Linux with glancekey-agent set-wait 5m.

On a Mac

In the GlanceKey menu, click Turn on… next to sudo and enter an administrator password. After pairing another iPhone or updating GlanceKey, the menu offers Update…. Turn off… puts sudo back to just your password.

If sudo ever stops working, this undoes it without sudo:

osascript -e 'do shell script "rm /etc/pam.d/sudo_local" with administrator privileges'

On Linux

sudo "$(command -v glancekey-agent)" enable-sudo    # again after pairing another iPhone or updating
sudo "$(command -v glancekey-agent)" disable-sudo   # back to just your password

It lists the iPhones it will trust, each with its key and the date it was paired, and asks first. Every iPhone is called "iPhone", so check that the key matches the one GlanceKey on your iPhone shows on the computer's page. The full path is there because sudo doesn't look in ~/.local/bin on Debian, Ubuntu and Fedora.

polkit keeps its settings in /usr/lib/pam.d on Arch and Debian, so enable-sudo copies them to /etc/pam.d/polkit-1; run it again after polkit updates.

Why it's safe

Your iPhone signs the command it showed you, and a part of GlanceKey that only the system can change checks that signature against the iPhones you paired. GlanceKey itself, the relay and other programs running as you only pass messages along, so they can't approve in your place.

On Linux this is mostly a convenience: your sudo password is your login password, and a program running as you has ways to catch it, for example the next time it's typed.

Mac password dialogs

When a Mac dialog asks for your password, such as 1Password, Safari passwords, System Settings or an installer, it shows up on your iPhone. Tap Approve and look at your phone, and GlanceKey types your saved password into that dialog. Deny cancels it. Handle the dialog on the Mac instead and the request disappears from your phone. Switch it off with Fill in password dialogs from iPhone in the menu.

Lock Screen card

Your iPhone shows your computers on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island: which are locked, and requests like sudo. Tap Unlock or Lock next to a computer, or Approve next to a request. These open GlanceKey, which checks your face and does it straight away. To deny a request, tap it to see it in the app.

It shows up when you open GlanceKey, and again when a computer locks or asks for something. To turn it off, switch off Show on the Lock Screen in the app. A computer running an older version of GlanceKey shows what the app saw when you last opened it.

Notifications

In the iPhone app, turn on Notify me. When a computer waits for you, the Lock Screen card lights up your iPhone with the request. Without the card, you get a notification that names the computer; tap it to open GlanceKey. What's waiting travels over the relay when the app asks, never in that notification.

Your own relay

The relay that connects your iPhone to your computers is one small Cloudflare Worker, in the relay folder of GlanceKey's code. In relay/wrangler.jsonc, remove account_id and replace the two routes with your own domain (or remove them for a workers.dev address). Then run npm install and npx wrangler deploy in that folder, and point your computers at it:

# Mac: then quit and reopen GlanceKey
defaults write nl.dinkelworks.glancekey.mac relayURL https://relay.example.com

# Linux: set "relay" in ~/.config/glancekey/agent.json, then
systemctl --user restart glancekey-agent

Then pair again. The QR code carries the relay's address, so your iPhone follows. Notifications need your own build of the iPhone app and your own Apple push key, because Apple only delivers them to apps of the key's developer. Computers on your own relay have no limits in the iPhone app.

Updating and removing

Mac

Update by downloading GlanceKey again and replacing it, or with brew upgrade --cask glancekey. If you use sudo approvals, click Update… when the menu asks for it.

To remove it: click Turn off… for sudo approvals, quit GlanceKey and delete it (or brew uninstall --cask glancekey), then remove the computer in the iPhone app.

Linux

Update by running the install command again, or with brew upgrade glancekey-agent followed by glancekey-agent install-service. If you use sudo approvals, run sudo "$(command -v glancekey-agent)" enable-sudo again.

To remove it:

sudo "$(command -v glancekey-agent)" disable-sudo
glancekey-agent forget-password
systemctl --user disable --now glancekey-agent
rm ~/.local/bin/glancekey-agent ~/.config/systemd/user/glancekey-agent.service   # or: brew uninstall glancekey-agent
rm -r ~/.config/glancekey

Troubleshooting

  • The computer shows as offline: check its internet connection; GlanceKey reconnects by itself. A sleeping computer can't be reached.
  • The Mac doesn't unlock: check that the menu shows Accessibility access and a saved password.
  • sudo asks for the password straight away: sudo approvals are off, you're connected over SSH, or the iPhone was paired after you turned them on (Update… on a Mac, enable-sudo again on Linux).
  • What happened: the Mac menu shows recent activity; on Linux, see journalctl --user -u glancekey-agent, and journalctl -t glancekey for sudo.
