Privacy policy

Last updated 28 October 2026

Short version: GlanceKey doesn’t collect personal data. There are no accounts, analytics, ads or trackers, in the apps or on this website. Your password never leaves your computer.

Who we are

GlanceKey is made by Ruben Dinkelman in the Netherlands. For questions about privacy, email ruben@dinkelworks.nl.

What stays on your devices

  • On your iPhone: the keys that sign your approvals and open the password saved on a Linux computer, kept in your iPhone’s secure hardware, which never lets them out; the list of computers you paired; how many unlocks and approvals you used today, for the free version’s daily limit; and, if you turn notifications on, its notification address.
  • On your computer: your login password, in the Mac keychain, or on Linux encrypted so that only your paired iPhone can open it (until each paired iPhone has unlocked once with the current app, a copy also stays in your keyring); the public keys of the iPhones you paired; the secret the computer uses to connect to the relay; if you turn notifications on, your iPhone’s notification address; and, if you show the Lock Screen card, the card’s address on the relay.

None of this is sent to us.

The relay

Your iPhone and your computers talk through a relay at relay.glancekey.com, which runs on Cloudflare. All traffic to it is encrypted.

  • What passes through: the messages between your iPhone and your computers, such as whether a computer is locked, unlock commands, and approval requests, which include the command or dialog that’s waiting for you. If you turn notifications on, also your iPhone’s notification address and the name of the computer that’s waiting. If you show the Lock Screen card, also what it shows (see below). Unlocking and approving are signed by your iPhone and checked by your computer, so the relay can’t fake or change them. It could decline a request or stop passing messages on.
  • What it stores: one thing per computer, a one-way fingerprint of the secret the computer uses to claim its place on the relay. The messages themselves aren’t stored, apart from what your Lock Screen card shows, while you have it on (see below).
  • Cloudflare: like any network, Cloudflare processes connection data such as IP addresses to deliver traffic, and keeps the relay’s request logs for a few days for troubleshooting. See Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

You can run your own relay instead. Then nothing passes through ours.

The subscription

If you subscribe to GlanceKey Unlimited, Apple handles the payment under its own privacy policy. The app asks Apple on your iPhone whether your subscription is active, and nothing about it is sent to us or to the relay. Apple’s sales reports tell us how many people subscribe in each country, not who they are.

A few times a day the app asks the relay what the free version’s limits are. That request says nothing about you or your computers.

Notifications

Notifications are optional. If you turn them on, Apple gives your iPhone an address for notifications, which your iPhone passes to the computers you paired. When a computer waits for your approval, the relay asks Apple to deliver a notification to that address, and doesn’t keep either. The notification only contains the computer’s name. With the Lock Screen card on, the card lights up your iPhone instead (see below). Apple’s privacy policy applies to its delivery.

Lock Screen card

The card on your Lock Screen is optional, and on unless you turn it off in the app. To keep it up to date while the app is closed, the relay keeps what the card shows and asks Apple to deliver each change to your iPhone:

  • the names of your paired computers and whether each is locked or offline, until you turn the card off;
  • a request waiting for your approval, including its command or dialog, until it’s answered or expires, which takes at most two minutes;
  • the addresses Apple gives your iPhone for the card, a random key the card’s buttons carry, and, if you turn notifications on, your iPhone’s notification address, for when there’s no card to show a request.

Turning the card off deletes all of it from the relay. Apple’s privacy policy applies to the delivery.

Crash reports

If you chose to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may pass anonymous crash reports on to us. You can turn this off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

This website

This website has no cookies, analytics or JavaScript. Cloudflare serves it and processes connection data such as IP addresses to do so.

Changes

If GlanceKey ever starts handling data differently, this page changes first, with a new date at the top.
